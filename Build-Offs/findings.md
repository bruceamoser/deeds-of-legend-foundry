# What the Build-Offs reveal

These are construction findings from thirteen legal HoL level-1 characters, not combat-playtest results. The paired D&D characters are level 2. Full choices and source references live in each comparison; the importable HoL files let a group test the findings directly.

## Trope coverage

| Trope | Current HoL route | Assessment of this build |
|---|---|---|
| Barbarian | Unbalanced, Arms, Athletics | Strong rage/bruiser identity. Resistance wording needs a ruling; rage has a different availability/tradeoff pattern. |
| Bard | Leader, Influence, Mentalism | Strong supporter, performer and social illusionist. Aid replaces a stored inspiration die; this selection lacks healing. |
| Cleric | Shepherd, Life, Wards | Strong healer/warder. Specific undead-turning and independent divine-channel resources are absent from this selection. |
| Druid | Shepherd, Life, Elements, Wilderness | Nature magic is strong; animal transformation is a substantial level-1 gap. Alter Self is not Wild Shape. |
| Fighter | Blade, Arms, Armor, Guard | Strong weapon specialist/protector. No selected Action Surge/Second Wind equivalent; heavy armor is intentionally later. |
| Monk | Blade, Arms, Athletics | Partial: a convincing mobile staff fighter, not yet an unarmed supernatural martial artist. |
| Paladin | Protector, Arms, Life, Force | Strong protector/healer fiction; partial execution of the familiar smiting knight. Sustained Magic Weapon is not burst smiting. |
| Ranger | Warden, Marksmanship, Wilderness, Elements | Strong archer/explorer with practical nature magic. Steady Aim rewards standing still rather than marking a mobile quarry. |
| Rogue | Shadow, Subterfuge, Athletics | Strong stealth, lockwork and opportunistic melee identity. Its attack replaces a basic attack rather than adding a separate damage die. |
| Sorcerer | Arcanist, Elements, Mentalism, Spellcraft | Spell-shaping fantasy works; innate-versus-studied identity mostly rests on signature and fiction. Mental-stat incentives differ. |
| Warlock | Odd, Invocation, Mentalism, Spellcraft, Force | Occult bargain, spirits and cantrip magic work. Pact-resource cadence and a continuously improving signature blast remain gaps. |
| Wizard | Arcanist, Scholarship, Divination, Force, Spellcraft | Strong learned utility mage. Fixed spellbook pairs replace open-ended copying and individual spell preparation. |
| Artificer | Intellect, Craft, Divination, Force | Strong repairer/magical improvisor. Temporary spell effects do not reproduce a persistent replicated-item loadout. |

“Strong” does not mean every class feature is reproduced. “Partial” does not mean the character is weak. These judgments concern what the player repeatedly gets to do.

| Bard | 12 / 12 | 14 | 7 | 10 |
| Cleric | 10 / 9 | 17 | 6 | 8 |
| Druid | 10 / 10 | 14 | 7 | 14 |
| Fighter | 8 / 8 | 16 | 3 | 0 |
| Monk | 7 / 7 | 16 | 3 | 0 |
| Paladin | 8 / 8 | 16 | 6 | 4 |
| Ranger | 8 / 8 | 15 | 5 | 4 |
| Rogue | 9 / 9 | 13 | 7 | 0 |

| Warlock | 9 / 9 | 15 | 10 | 10 |
| Wizard | 11 / 11 | 13 | 10 | 12 |
| Artificer | 10 / 10 | 13 | 9 | 8 |

The physical specialists have fewer DP than the broad mental specialists, as intended. The Barbarian can deepen one attack fully before the level-3 endpoint and still take a movement choice and a defensive Reaction. That is a workable small kit. The Bard can invest broadly in social skills and magic. None of these builds depends on Adaptable for an extra DP.

Class prices now matter. The Cleric spends 3 DP to add Armor and 1 more for Light Armor. The Artificer spends 2 DP to add Force. The Wizard adds Spellcraft for 1 DP. Those are real sacrifices, not merely different labels on an identical build.

However, a free discipline from an origin can save between 1 and 3 DP depending on class. That makes origins mechanically valuable as well as descriptive. Test whether players feel free to select a believable history or feel pushed into shopping for the most expensive free discipline. This is a consequence of the approved model, not a reason to silently remove class prices.

## The simplification goal needs a separate spell test

A fighter’s improved attack remains one play choice even after several purchases. Spellbook steps are different: the Druid knows fourteen spells, and several other level-1 casters know ten or twelve, before universal actions and the signature. This preserves broad magical utility, but can recreate the original decision-paralysis problem if every spell is placed on the main sheet.

Keep the complete spellbooks. For the playtest, give each build a short favorites set and test whether the player can find a situational spell quickly in its book. Measure selection time and mistakes as well as satisfaction. A simpler creation list reduces purchase friction; it does not by itself reduce combat decision load.

## Attribute incentives should be tested explicitly

The Wizard’s Knowledge improves its casting rolls, Arcanist SP and starting DP. The Sorcerer comparison instead prioritizes Guile to preserve its charismatic identity, paying for capacity to offset weaker Knowledge-based SP. The Warlock’s Odd class uses Guile for SP, but Arcane Bolt uses Knowledge and several occult spells use Reason. Its broad reserve is not equivalent to a strong attack roll.

That creates a genuine choice, but it can make a charismatic magic-user feel punished for fulfilling the trope. Decide whether that cost is desirable before changing spell attributes or introducing class-specific casting substitutions. Do not grant such substitutions just to make these comparisons look closer.

## Match play loops before matching feature names

- **Druid:** the earliest transformation spells adapt the body or change appearance; they do not provide a beast stat block. If becoming an animal is a first-session expectation, it needs an explicit capability at the intended gate, not a generous reading of Alter Self.
- **Monk:** a staff, movement Maneuver and counterattack create a mobile martial artist. The collection still lacks a supported unarmed progression and its distinctive rapid-strike/focus loop. A purpose-built branch may solve this more cleanly than a new class.
- **Paladin:** healing, interposition and weapon blessing support an oath-bound champion. A burst attack that spends magic through a weapon is a distinct capability; choose whether it belongs in a branch, spellbook or signature.
- **Artificer:** Craft plus Force covers construction and temporary enchantment. A persistent item-production loop requires rules for duration, ownership, limits and replacement. A renamed Magic Weapon spell does not supply those rules.
- **Warlock:** the spirit bargain is easy to express. The small renewable Pact Magic pool is not: Recovery I restores only 1 SP once between safe overnight rests. Keep this difference visible rather than adding a short-rest refresh by analogy.

These are directions for design discussion, not approved new mechanics.

## Review decisions to settle before the book pass

The [trait audit](ancestry-trait-review.md) and [signature audit](class-signature-review.md) contain the complete evidence. The most consequential questions are:

1. Which magical signatures count as spells for sustain, casting triggers and counter-magic?
2. Should later rage signatures deliberately depend on Go Berserk, or should each milestone choice stand alone?
3. What damage type does Go Berserk’s DR cover? How should Arcane Deflection determine its push distance?
4. What is the exact action allowance for commanding Call the Wild’s real animal?
5. Which ancestry trait disparities are intentional after accounting for ancestry HP/SP/coins and Human borrowing?
6. Which trope gaps should be available immediately, and which are deliberately reserved for later development?

There are also straightforward editorial corrections: Adaptable’s level-0 reference, Mark the Quarry’s “if it hits,” the magic introduction’s flat discipline price, and the recovery reference’s old starting-SP formula. They are recorded as findings here, not silently patched into the comparison baseline.

## Suggested table trial

Use the same three situations for each pair: an exposed ally and a blocking enemy; an obstacle that rewards that hero’s utility specialty; and a second encounter before full recovery. Ask the player to choose a main action, movement/support option and response to danger without coaching. Record time to choose, rule lookups, resources spent, and whether the player felt like the intended hero.

Avoid a winner based on raw HP or damage. D&D hit probability, HoL’s always-hit attacks, defensive rolls, resistance, action economy and rest cadence are too different for those numbers alone to establish parity. The present documents establish legal builds and identify what to test next.
